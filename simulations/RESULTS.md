# MARV V2 power simulation results
Status: PASS

Generated by `python3 tools/run_power_checks.py` with ngspice. These are assumption-based models, not vendor IC simulations or hardware signoff.

## Integrated source/load model

Power comes from 5V_IN -- the output of U26, the on-board AP63205WU buck that makes a fixed 5.0 V (4.95/5.00/5.05 V, DS41326 Rev. 2-2) from the 6-25.2 V pack on the ESC pad row J3, modeled as a 5.0 V source behind 0.10 ohm of effective output impedance plus a 2 A hard current limit (the rated continuous output of the part; its high-side peak current limit is 2.5 A min / 2.8 A typ / 3.1 A max), behavioral rather than a vendor regulator model, with the pack and the buck conversion itself outside these decks -- or from USB (J4, a plain 5.0 V rail through 0.35 ohm cable/connector resistance, no current limit -- USB compliance/negotiation is a firmware/operational matter, not modeled here). Both enter ONE U25 TPS2121 priority power mux: IN1 = 5V_IN has priority, IN2 = USB is the fallback, OUT = V5_SYS. It is modeled by the muxctl subckt (the shared selection logic of datasheet Table 9-3 for the CP2 < VREF rows the board uses, with the UV / OV1 / PR1 / VCOMP comparators and their hysteresis) plus one muxpath subckt per channel (RON 56 mOhm typ, tSW 100 us, and always-on reverse-current blocking with IRCB 1 A / tRCB 10 us / VRCB 25 mV and NO body diode -- see the subckt comments and simulations/README.md for what this can and cannot show). V5_SYS bulk capacitance is C8+C9 2 x 10 uF ceramic (14 uF derated in lossy_path) plus C19 220 uF polymer with 40 mOhm ESR (176 uF derated); the 5V_IN node itself now carries 44.1 uF (C76+C77+C71, the AP63205 COUT plus the mux IN1 bypass; the old ~110 uF XT30 input network is gone with the 5 V input) and USB_VBUS ~1.1 uF (C7+C72). U7 TPS62913 (buck, V5_SYS->V3V3_SYS, 92% assumed efficiency, dropout-clamped so VOUT <= VIN-0.15 V) carries the RP2354B digital domains, QSPI flash, microSD and the peripheral ports; U12 TPS7A20 (LDO, V5_SYS->V3V3_ANA, VOUT = min(3.3, VIN-0.2 V), ideal pass-through current) carries the MEMS sensors and the RP2354B ADC_AVDD pin. The nominal corner times 5V_IN present 5-40 ms and USB present 30-55 ms, so each is the sole source for a window (5V_IN alone 5-30 ms, USB alone 40-55 ms) and both overlap 30-40 ms -- and in that overlap the mux now keeps IN1, which is the behaviour the LM66100 pair could not guarantee. Load steps 500->800->500 mA on V3V3_SYS per the budget, with a constant 10 mA on V3V3_ANA. Predicates: V3V3_SYS in the 3.135-3.60 V window and V5_SYS >= 3.5 V whenever a source is present; V3V3_ANA >= 3.135 V. usb_only and vbat_only remove one source for the whole run (the other source's own presence window is then the evaluation window); lossy_path adds 50% to both source resistances on top of the derated caps.

Modeled converter loss at 500 mA (new nominal continuous load, from the nominal corner's steady 500 mA plateau): buck 0.144 W, LDO 0.016 W. At 300 mA (separate constant-load run): buck 0.086 W, LDO 0.017 W. ## Source handover, over-voltage and mis-plug with TPS2121 thresholds

Corners below drive simulations/source_handover.cir directly: a behavioral TPS2121 (datasheet SLVSEA3F -- RON 56 mOhm typ / 100 mOhm max, tSW 100 us for the CP2 < VREF mode the board uses, reverse-current blocking IRCB 1 A typ / 2 A max, tRCB 10 us, VRCB 25 mV typ / 50 mV max, VUV 2.65/2.55 V, VCOMP 280 mV) feeding the same TPS62913-class buck as integrated_power.cir (setpoint 3.33 V, 92% efficiency, vmin 3.0 V, dropout-clamped). There is no cell/battery path; the SURVIVOR (present for the whole run) and the OTHER source (removed, inserted, or held over-voltage at t=15 ms) are 5V_IN and USB, whichever the case assigns, and a third substitutable ROLE block tells the controller which of them is IN1. Both sources sit at their nominal 5.0 V: the LM66100 deck this replaces had to split them 4.6 V / 5.4 V to make the pre-event state well-defined because that part had no priority mechanism, whereas the TPS2121 decides from PR1/OV1/VCOMP, so the split is gone and near-equal sources are now the case actually tested. Two board-derived thresholds carry the OV corners: the OV1 divider R44 45.3k / R45 10k puts the datasheet VREF (1.06 V rising, 1.04 V falling) at 5.862 V / 5.751 V on 5V_IN, and the PR1 divider R42 32.4k / R43 10k puts it at 4.494 V / 4.410 V (centred on the rising edge so the 4.664 V worst-case rising threshold still sits below the 4.75 V minimum declared input). Each channel model has NO body diode -- the TPS2121 is back-to-back FETs with always-on reverse blocking (Sec 9.3.6) -- so the hold-up capacitance, not a forward-biased diode, carries the load across the 100 us switchover; that is strictly harder than the LM66100 case it replaces. The 5V_IN node carries its real 44.1 uF (C76+C77+C71) and USB_VBUS its real ~1.1 uF (C7+C72); that is load-bearing, not cosmetic, because a token 100 nF source cap has too little charge for reverse blocking to separate the rails and the model latches half-on in a state the hardware cannot reach. Each row's precondition column reports the state just before the 15 ms event and what it had to be for the row to mean anything: for vin5_removed_usb_present the removed 5V_IN must have been established (>= 4.5 V) and the USB channel must have been held OFF by IN1's priority (conduction < 0.1); for usb_removed_vin5_present the surviving 5V_IN channel must have been ON (> 0.9) and the USB channel OFF; for usb_insert_vin5_present and the over-voltage corners the survivor-fed sys rail must already have been running (>= 3.1 V, i.e. buck vmin 3.0 V + margin). Otherwise the row is marked INVALID rather than reporting a meaningless measurement. Every threshold in the state path is a tanh rather than a min/max clamp so the solver never has to step across a discontinuity at the handover instant. This is not a vendor IC simulation: no gate/loop dynamics, no current limiting, no thermal shutdown, no SS-programmed startup slew and no bench measurement are represented. VSYS bulk is 2 x 10 uF ceramic (14 uF derated) plus C19 220 uF polymer (176 uF at -20%) with 40 mOhm ESR. Predicates: V5_SYS >= 3.5 V and V3V3_SYS in the 3.135-3.60 V window, evaluated from 2 ms (well after startup) through the end of the 30 ms run; the two over-voltage corners add V5_SYS <= 5.6 V and IN1 conduction < 0.1 over that whole window.

The "Other-channel max state" column is the peak conduction fraction of the OTHER source's channel over the whole 2-30 ms evaluation window. For vin5_removed_usb_present and the two over-voltage corners the OTHER source is 5V_IN = IN1, so that column is the IN1 rejection evidence (it must stay below 0.1 in the over-voltage corners); for usb_removed_vin5_present and usb_insert_vin5_present the OTHER source is USB = IN2, and 0.000 there means the fallback channel never turned on because IN1 kept priority. A negative reverse peak means even the maximum of the survivor source current is still forward, i.e. no reverse current occurred at any point.

## Reverse current into the 5V_IN path (TPS2121 reverse-blocking region)

One corner built from source_handover.cir's hooks: USB inserted onto a V5_SYS already running from a present 5V_IN, with no source removal (5V_IN stays present for the whole run), at the worst-case device numbers (RON 100 mOhm, IRCB 2 A, VRCB 50 mV) so the most charge possible moves before reverse blocking completes. This is the corner the LM66100 could not pass by construction -- its reverse current below VOFF was simply not blocked -- whereas the TPS2121 has reverse-current blocking always on for both channels. Reporting-only: no pass/fail predicate is applied.

| usb_insert_onto_5vin | worst-case RON=0.100 / IRCB=2 / VRCB=0.050 | 0.50 A | 0.0000 A | 4.927 V | 1.000 | -0.365 A | 0.0000 ms |

A near-zero standing reverse current here is the behaviour the TPS2121 was chosen for: IN1 keeps priority, the IN2 channel never turns on, and neither input can be back-fed from V5_SYS. See DESIGN_SPEC.md.


## Enclosure sensitivity — the load also heats the board

Assume 60 C surrounding environment and 10 J/K effective board heat capacity. Loss inputs are buck+LDO conversion heat only. 300 mA row: 0.99 W electronics heat (the P3 load budget's typical share) plus 0.103 W conversion heat. 500 mA row: 1.3 W onboard electronics heat (external ELRS/GNSS modules dissipate off-board) plus 0.160 W conversion heat. Whole-assembly thermal resistance is unknown: the following values are scenarios, not PCB estimates. These temperatures are the lumped board node, not regulator junction temperatures.

| Load | Assumed assembly thermal resistance | Board at 10 min | Board at 60 min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 300 mA | 10 K/W | 70.9 C | 70.9 C |
| 300 mA | 20 K/W | 80.8 C | 81.9 C |
| 300 mA | 40 K/W | 94.0 C | 103.7 C |
| 500 mA | 10 K/W | 74.6 C | 74.6 C |
| 500 mA | 20 K/W | 87.7 C | 89.2 C |
| 500 mA | 40 K/W | 105.4 C | 118.4 C |

The weak 40 K/W cooling scenario exceeds the provisional 100 C junction target even at the board node. Low-loss converters alone cannot guarantee acceptable temperature in a sealed enclosure. Measure enclosure coupling, board copper and local regulator gradients; reduce load or add a conductive heat path as needed.

## Remaining validation

- Vendor-model or bench checks for regulator loops, startup, current-limit behavior and source handover; the generic models do not cover these.
- USB enumeration/startup/inrush/suspend and higher-current control, including tolerance extremes.
- Real input transients, TVS/fuse coordination and reverse-current pulses.
- Logging and actuator-disturbance tests, and no-airflow enclosure thermal soak.
- Final MPNs, effective capacitance, inductor saturation/temperature and PCB layout.
